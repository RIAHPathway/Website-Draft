### ADDITIONAL TYPICAL DURATIONS, ACCELERATION, AND STATE LAW-STUDY EXAMPLES

[TEXT — ACCESSIBLE AND AFFORDABLE PACING; RIGOR IS NOT REDUCED]

Typical GED/HSE **preparation** takes approximately **one year**, depending on readiness; the High School Diploma typically takes **four years**; an Associate's **two years**; a Bachelor's **four years**; a Minor **one semester to one year**; a Master's **one year**; an MBA **one year**; and the RIAH J.D. pathway typically **four years**.

Eligible educational pathways from GED/HSE preparation through J.D. and state-specific Non-J.D. education may offer accelerated **internal coursework**. The earliest potential internal curriculum completion is one month, but only after all required courses or approved transferred equivalents, prerequisites, applicable objective and performance assessments at the **80% minimum**, assigned review courses at the **80% internal minimum**, proctored examinations, projects, documented supervision hours, and capstones are completed and verified. Applicable attendance, residency, state graduation requirements, accreditation standards, minimum periods of study, and external examinations remain controlling. One month is **not** a guaranteed degree, diploma, license, or official GED/HiSET credential; acceleration makes education more accessible, not easier.

State Non-J.D. rules vary. California's Law Office Study route requires **four years** of approved supervised study. Maine's statutory route requires **at least one year** in a Maine attorney's office **after** completion of two-thirds of an ABA-accredited law school's degree requirements. Maine's one-year legal-study segment is not a standalone one-year law program. A J.D. cannot be conferred in one month when applicable institutional, state, or ABA minimum-time rules prohibit it.

**No acceleration:** Professional Experiential and Entrepreneurship Experiential retain their published fixed durations, supervision, milestones, and applied capstones. **Review courses** including certification, Bar Review, and Baby Bar preparation may be completed at the student's own pace when all course and assessment requirements are met; external testing and licensing remain separate.

### ACCELERATION

One month is the earliest potential curriculum-completion timeframe where permitted by the applicable program requirements.

It is not a guaranteed degree or credential completion period.

Acceleration does not remove:

- Prerequisites.
- Required coursework.
- Objective assessments.
- Performance assessments.
- Applicable projects.
- Applicable midterms.
- Applicable finals.
- Required proctoring.
- Capstones.
- Professional supervision.
- Payment requirements.
- Mandatory duration requirements.
- External credentialing requirements.


Acceleration changes academic pacing, not curriculum obligations. Applicable simulations, assigned curriculum products and learning-resource controls, experiential experience requirements, and approved course-level supervision remain in force when a student progresses faster.
